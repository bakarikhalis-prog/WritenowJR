# WriteNow Jr.

A writing app for students in grades 6 to 8. It teaches three kinds of writing:

- **A story**: made-up stories, short or long (narrative writing)
- **A true story**: something that really happened (personal narrative)
- **Explain it**: how-to pieces, reports and opinions (informational and argument writing)

Each kind has its own course of short lessons. Every lesson explains one idea, shows an example, names a common mistake, and ends with one small task the student does right there in the app. By the end of the course, the student has planned, written and finished a short piece of their own.

It runs entirely on the device. No accounts, no sign-in, no internet connection after the first visit, and no data collection of any kind. Nothing a student writes leaves the device unless they copy or save it themselves.

## Install it on the Home Screen

**Do this before writing anything.** On iPhone and iPad, Safari erases the storage of any site that hasn't been visited in seven days. An app installed on the Home Screen is exempt from that rule. Used in a plain browser tab, work can disappear; installed, it stays.

### iPhone and iPad

1. Open the link in **Safari**. Other browsers can't do this step.
2. Tap the **Share** button: the square with an arrow pointing up.
3. Scroll down and tap **Add to Home Screen**.
4. Tap **Add**.

Open it from the Home Screen icon from now on, not from Safari.

### Chromebook, Windows and Mac

1. Open the link in **Chrome** or **Edge**.
2. Click the **install icon** in the address bar (a monitor with a downward arrow).
3. Click **Install**.

If you don't see the icon, use the **⋮** menu → **Cast, save and share** → **Install page as app**.

### Android

1. Open the link in **Chrome**.
2. Tap the **⋮** menu, then **Add to Home screen** or **Install app**.

## For teachers

Open **Settings → For teachers** inside the app. You'll find:

- **A classroom guide**: pacing, privacy, how students hand in work, and what to know about shared devices.
- **Printable lesson sheets**: one page per lesson for each of the three courses, matching the lessons in the app exactly. Each sheet has the idea, an example, what to watch out for, the task, and lines for notes. They download as a file that opens in any browser; print from there.

**Pacing.** One or two lessons fit a class period with time to write. The app checks most tasks itself and moves each student on when the task is done, so a class can work at different speeds.

**Handing in work.** In the Book tab, students can copy their whole piece to paste into a document or an email, or save it as a file that prints cleanly. From the books button at the top, **Export this book** saves a single book as a file you can collect.

**Shared devices.** The first time WriteNow Jr. opens, it asks who uses the device. Choose **Students take turns** for a class set, a library computer or a shared iPad. You can switch it later under Settings → For teachers. On a shared device:

- The app opens on a start screen. Students tap **Open my work** and pick the file they saved last time, or **I'm new here** and type their first name and last initial.
- Everything comes back from the file: their writing, which lesson they're on, their writing days and the walkthroughs they've already seen.
- When they're done, they tap **Save & finish** at the top of the screen. The app saves one file with their name on it, asks them to check it's there, then clears the device so the next student starts fresh and can't see anything.
- If a student leaves without finishing, the next student sees a notice and can save that work to the device as a file for you before starting.

Have students save their file somewhere that's theirs: their school Google Drive or OneDrive, a USB stick, or a class folder you collect. On a shared device the Downloads folder is shared too. On Chromebooks where students sign in with their own school account, each account already keeps its own work, so shared mode isn't needed there.

**Safety.** The lessons are written for this age group. The true-story lessons encourage students to choose memories they are comfortable sharing, to be fair to real people, and to talk to a parent, teacher or school counselor before writing about something that still feels hard or scary.

## After installing

**It works offline.** Once it has loaded, students can write with no connection.

**It updates itself.** Open it with a connection and you get the current version.

**Back up anyway.** Settings → Backup & Restore → Copy backup. A device wipe or a browser reset erases the app's storage along with everything else. The backup is one file you keep.

**WriteNow Jr. and WriteNow are separate.** They keep their work apart, even on the same device. A backup from one can be restored into the other, so a student can move up later and keep everything.
